Government portals are the unsung enemy of every job applicant, college student,
and visa applicant. They demand a PDF, then reject it because it's "too large".
The size limits aren't documented anywhere consistent - you find out by uploading,
waiting, and failing.

This guide collects the file-size rules for the most common portals worldwide and
shows the fastest way to hit each target.

## Common portals and their limits

### Bangladesh

| Portal | PDF/photo size | Signature |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BCS (BPSC) | 100 KB | 60 KB |
| NID Wing | 100–200 KB | 40 KB |
| Passport e-Application | 150 KB | 50 KB |
| University admissions | 1 MB | 200 KB |

### India

| Portal | PDF/photo size | Signature |
| --- | --- | --- |

## Why portals enforce limits

Most portals were designed when bandwidth and storage were expensive. The limits
have never been relaxed because:

- They keep the database small and queryable
- They force users to upload "lighter" scans, reducing OCR cost
- They reject malware payloads (which tend to be large)

The limits aren't going anywhere, so we built tools for them.

## Tips for cleaner scans

Before compressing, you can shrink the original by:

- Scanning at **200 DPI**, not 600 (forms only need 200)
- Using **grayscale** or **B&W** instead of color
- Cropping out white margins before scanning

A 200 DPI grayscale scan of an A4 form is often **already 100 KB** without any
compression.

## Common mistakes

- **Compressing twice** - repeated compression amplifies artifacts. Always start from the original.
- **Wrong target** - if 100 KB makes text unreadable, try 200 KB. Most portals accept slightly larger files than their documented cap.
- **Combining BCS docs into one PDF** - many portals want each document as a separate PDF.
