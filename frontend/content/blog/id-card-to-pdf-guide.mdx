Most official processes that ask for a copy of your ID expect both sides on a single
page. Banks, HR departments, visa offices, and school registrars all want the same
thing: front and back, clearly readable, printed on one sheet of paper.

The problem is that doing this manually is annoying. Print the front on one page, then
reload the paper, then print the back — and hope the alignment works. Or paste images
into a Word document and fight with margins for twenty minutes.

The [ID Card to PDF tool](/tools/id-card-to-pdf) handles this in under a minute.

## When You Need This

The two-sided ID PDF comes up constantly:

**Passport renewal.** Many countries require a copy of your current passport — both
the photo page and the data page — submitted alongside the renewal application.

**KYC (Know Your Customer) verification.** Banks, fintech apps, crypto exchanges, and
investment platforms all require identity verification during account opening. They
want both sides of a government-issued ID in a single document.

**Employee onboarding.** HR departments collecting ID for payroll, tax forms, and
compliance record-keeping typically ask for a scanned copy of a national ID or
driver's license — front and back on one page.

**School enrollment.** Universities and schools in many countries require ID copies
when admitting students or processing financial aid.

**Visa applications.** Embassies and consulates requesting supporting documents
often specify that ID copies should be single-page, with both faces visible.

## Common ID Types

The tool works with any rectangular ID card:

- **Driver's license** — most countries issue two-sided licenses with photo on front,
  vehicle class and restrictions on back
- **National ID card** — government-issued identity documents used across Europe,
  Asia, Africa, and Latin America
- **Passport data page** — the biographical page containing photo, MRZ, and personal
  details
- **Employee badge** — corporate or institutional ID cards with access information
  on the reverse
- **Business card** — while not an identity document, the same layout tools work for
  printing business card content on A4

## 3-Step Walkthrough

**Step 1: Upload front and back.**
Open the [ID Card to PDF tool](/tools/id-card-to-pdf). You'll see two upload zones:
one labelled "Front" and one labelled "Back." Drop an image (JPG, PNG) or a scanned
PDF into each slot. Smartphone photos work fine — the tool accepts common image
formats and adjusts for rotation.

**Step 2: Choose your layout.**
Three layout options are available:

- **A4 portrait (stacked)** — The front card appears in the upper half of a vertical
  A4 page, the back card in the lower half. This is the most widely accepted format
  for official submissions.

- **A4 landscape (side-by-side)** — Both cards appear on a horizontal A4 page, front
  on the left, back on the right. Useful when the cards are wide-format or when the
  recipient's form specifies landscape orientation.

- **Compact** — Both cards are placed with more whitespace around them, slightly
  smaller. Helpful when the receiving system shrinks PDFs and you want to ensure the
  card text remains readable after scaling.

**Step 3: Download.**
Click **Generate PDF**. The download starts immediately — a clean, single-page A4
PDF with both card images at accurate proportions. No watermark, no banner, no
"upgrade to remove logo."

## Printing Tips

**Use "Actual size" in your print dialog, not "Fit to page."** Most PDF viewers
default to scaling the document to fit the paper. For ID cards, this changes the
card dimensions. Switch to "Actual size" (sometimes called "100%" or "No scaling")
to ensure the card prints at its real-world dimensions.

**Check your printer's margin settings.** Some printers apply a non-printable border
around the edge of the page. If the cards appear slightly cropped when printed, adjust
the margin in your printer settings or select "Borderless" if your printer supports it.

**Laser vs. inkjet.** Either works for document submission. For documents that will
be laminated or handled frequently, laser prints tend to be more durable. For
submissions that are scanned on receipt anyway (which most digital KYC processes do),
inkjet quality is sufficient.

Your ID images are transmitted over HTTPS, processed to generate the PDF layout, and
deleted from the server within one hour. The tool does not extract data from the card,
does not read the MRZ or chip, and does not store a copy. No account is required, so
there is no profile your upload can be linked to.

If you're working with a particularly sensitive document — a passport or government
ID — you can verify this by checking your browser's network tab to confirm the upload
endpoint and reviewing the privacy policy linked from the tool page.
