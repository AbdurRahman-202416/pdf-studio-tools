Editing a PDF in Word, Google Docs, or LibreOffice starts with one boring step:
turning the PDF back into a `.docx`. Most online converters either watermark the
output, ask for your email, or cap you at 5 MB.

PDF Studio's [PDF to Word](/pdf-to-word) tool is free, has no caps, doesn't ask
for an email, and runs entirely on our server (nothing installs on your machine).

## What gets preserved

- **Paragraphs and line breaks** - exactly as in the source
- **Headings** (when the PDF has a structural tag for them)
- **Basic tables** - rows and columns are reconstructed
- **Inline images** - extracted and re-embedded into the docx
- **Bold, italic, underline** - when the source uses real font weights (not just thick strokes)

## What needs cleanup

PDF is a print-layout format; Word is a reflowing-text format. A few things don't
round-trip cleanly:

- **Multi-column magazine layouts** end up as single columns in Word
- **Floating text boxes** (sidebars, callouts) are inlined where they appeared
- **Equations** become approximations - for math-heavy papers, keep the PDF

If your PDF is **scanned** (e.g. from a copier), run [PDF OCR](/pdf-ocr) first -
that adds a text layer, then PDF to Word can extract real, editable text.
